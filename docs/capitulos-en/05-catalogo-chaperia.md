# Hardware

## What Hardware Is

Hardware is gear that isn't implanted: weapons, armor, tools, drones, tactical clothing, data in a Neuraslot, etc. In street slang: if it isn't bolted into your body, it's hardware.

---

## Chrome vs hardware

| Aspect | Chrome |
| --- | --- |
| Where it sits | Integrated into the body |
| Affects @Psyche / humanity? | **Yes** |
| Variety | Fewer pieces; each one weighs a ton |
| At the table | "Instinctive" implant capabilities |

| Aspect | Hardware |
| --- | --- |
| Where it sits | Inventory, vehicle, pockets |
| Affects @Psyche / humanity? | **No** |
| Variety | Lots of variety; more accessible |
| At the table | You have to know how to handle the piece; walking around with an arsenal draws attention |

In short: weapons, tools, neurodata in slots, and clothing:

Many pieces demand technical know-how or integration with chrome (HUD + cyberoptics). Walking around with lethal hardware or EMPs in plain sight can backfire: it resolves in the fiction with the GM.

> **Example — Why walking around like a rolling arsenal is a bad idea**
> The Outsider crosses the checkpoint into the district with the assault rifle slung, the shotgun on his back, the Minimal missile launcher in his hand, and a tech armor shining under the neon. He says: "I walk in nice and easy — I haven't done anything." The GM smiles: "CM — and you've got nothing helping you." 2d6+CM: **5**.
>
> He picks the consequence: corporate patrol.
>
> "I walk three blocks and the district turns its back on me. Stalls pull their shutters down as I pass. A girl films me with her phone, not even hiding it. On the corner, two suits talk into their wrists, watching me. Before I reach the hotel, a corporate patrol cuts me off with the transport sideways: floodlights, loudspeaker, four helmets coming down with weapons in hand. 'Identification and carry permits.' Half an hour against the wall while they search me down to my fillings and 'hold' the missile launcher for verification. I got into the district free, sure. No missile launcher, tagged in three databases, and half the district knowing I arrived."
>
> Cut to: the Fixer crosses the same checkpoint an hour later in a street-grade corposuit —CM advantage— with a discreet pistol under the cut of his jacket. He greets the guard by name. They ask how his family's doing. Nobody films him. Nobody looks twice. In Night City, the arsenal that impresses is the one you can't see.

## Quick reminder

- Hardware adds no @Psyche.

- Standard-ammo pistol: legal in Night City; everything else needs a permit or going illegal (the usual).

- SAI = smartgun system (you need the smartgun link chrome piece, unless the weapon says otherwise).

- Accessories and subsystems: added without penalty; they only cost their Credits/Stars.

### Smartgun system (SAI)

| SAI | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Ionic accelerator | Projectiles/energy: **+1 damage** per hit (sniper rifle and missile launcher: **+3** where the weapon calls for it) |
| Assisted aiming | With a visual HUD: real-time target info. **Advantage on KR** |
| Ballistic flow | No reload times (ammo cycled by the SAI) |
| Stabilizer | Weapon usable **one-handed** (assault rifle; the sniper uses "stabilizer and decompressor") |

### Grenades

Sold in boxes of 8.

> The blast radius doesn't pick sides: allies, hostages, and bystanders are all in the fiction. Who ends up inside the reach is decided by what the scene already showed, not by the thrower's generosity. Throwing one changes the scene for everyone — including whoever wasn't part of the plan. And the noise stays in the city long after the smoke is gone.

| Type | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Acid** | **1d6** corrosive, **1 m** radius. If damage is **5+**, destroys a **second** chrome piece (automatic **−2** on rolls; mark zone damage with an "*") or a hardware piece of the thrower's choice |
| **Stun** | Kills organic hearing, cyberaudio, and ultrasonic radar for **1d6** turns, **10 m** radius. Detonation turn: whoever fails **MT** loses their action. The dB adjuster negates it |
| **Cryo** | **1d6** freezing, **3 m** radius; **1d6** turns at half movement; freezes/destroys living tissue; temporarily paralyzes chrome and hardware |
| **Frag** | **3d6** explosive, **5 m** radius |
| **Gas** | Toxic cloud **5 m**, **1d6** turns; **1d6** damage per turn inside the radius |
| **Smoke and particles** | Kills organic sight, cyberoptics, and EM radar for **1d6** turns, **5 m** radius. Breathing it: **6−** on MT loses the first turn. The toxin filter negates the breathing effect |
| **EMP** | Kills cybernetics and electronics within **5 m** for **1d6** turns |

> **Example — A SAI weapon in action**
> The Corpo is boxed in at the underground parking lot, three street toughs coming down the ramps. He has the smartgun link and a Hi-Tech assault rifle with two subsystems: assisted aiming and ballistic flow. "I cut off their way down." The GM: "KR — the HUD gives you advantage." 2d6+KR with advantage: **8**.
>
> The GM offers: you're left with no loads for whatever comes next, or the noise climbs and building security tags you, or one of the three is left alive and bleeding. He picks the noise.
>
> "The HUD paints all three in red before they round the corner: distance, pulse, cover. First shot manual, single, controlled: the front one drops with his chest open, a dry 1d6. The other two dive behind a van. I switch to semi and the ballistic flow does its magic: no reloading, no thinking, a three-round burst with advantage on KR and the second one crumples against the tire. The third runs up the ramp; I let him go. The rifle cools in my hands while the building's alarms light up floor by floor. I got out alive. And now all of corporate security has my ballistic signature."

> The Biohacker is in her container workshop, solder and fried-food smell in the air, and she needs to know how many guards are in the clandestine clinic across the street without leaving her chair. She pulls out her street-grade drone —two subsystems: watchman and silent— and sends it through the vents. "I walk it over the roof and map the clinic." The GM: "EN." 2d6+EN: **7**.
>
> The GM offers: the drone comes back hit and needs repairs, or someone picks up the control signal and triangulating it is just a matter of time, or the footage comes out incomplete, no basement. She takes the signal.
>
> "The drone slides through the duct without a sound —silent, nearly inaudible, you'd only hear it in a vacuum— and I watch everything through its camera on the workshop's broken screen. Watchman recording: two guards at reception, one asleep in the hallway, cameras on every corner, the OR door with a keypad. Five meters a turn, patient, let the drone breathe. I write it all on the whiteboard in marker. When I bring it back, a little light blinks on the analyzer: someone traced the control signal. They don't know where I am yet, but they're already looking. Hardware doesn't touch my @Psyche; it touches something else: luck. And mine just got tagged."

### Grapple gun

Pressure hook or magnet; the cable reels in (target toward you, or you toward the anchor point, depending on weight). Range 50 m. Can snag people.

> **Example — Grapple gun**
> The service stairs end at a welded door. Below, the patrol's lights are already rounding the corner. Dante, Mercenary, looks at the building across the street: fifty meters of empty air and an antenna that looks eager to help.
>
> —I shoot the grapple at the antenna and cross over the cable before they spot me.
>
> —RC —says the GM—: pulse, timing on the winch, and a cable you've never met. Roll.
>
> 2d6 + 1. Comes up **10**. The grapple bites the antenna on the first shot, the cable goes taut, and Dante crosses the alley flying low. Below, the patrol finds the welded door and starts arguing over whose fault it is.

### Mobile turret

Preloaded whitelist; fires at the nearest enemy: 1d6/turn. The turret's acting roll: 2d6+0.

> For the turret, "enemy" is anyone not on its whitelist — an ally without clearance counts as a threat. The turret's acting roll (2d6+0) belongs to the turret, not the players: use it when something contests its work (a sprint to cover, a live hack), not for every routine burst.

### Trauma card

Trauma Team membership in your pocket; monitors your biological and cybernetic systems. If you go down, an air transport arrives with biohackers and a military escort.

- Monthly payment; each activation burns the card (membership has to be renewed).

| Tier | Service |
| --- | --- |
| **Client** | Arrives in **15 min**; they drop you at your subscription address after stabilization |
| **Member** | **5 min** |
| **Preferred** | After **1 day** of therapy: healed and repaired to **50%** |
| **VIP** | They cover **100%** of costs and treatment time (health and cybernetics) |

## Neurodata (slot content)

Goes into Neuraslots (chrome). Different from the Neurodata you sell for Stars (session experience).

### Database

Variable content: code, networks, combat techniques to assist cyberware, VIP info, audio, etc. Advantage on NE, CM, KR, or MT when the task uses that data (hacking, mimicking a voice/face, speeches, holding your breath, etc.).

### Blank memory

Space to store data you download in play.

### Neuro-experience

An appraised recording of a lived experience; barter currency (your own are worth more). For learning or recreation.

### Veil protocol

Cancels the disadvantage of entering the old net and remote links.

- Implementing it is very hard, even for veteran netrunners.

- If you fail the implementation, you stay exposed to the old net's AIs.

- Double 1: the protocol deactivates; AIs can mine/invade the brain's processor → catastrophic consequences (GM's call).

### Ghost mask

Unrecognizable to optical systems (physical or holographic). CM advantage vs facial recognition. Doesn't fool anyone with the same capability.

> The mask lies to the cameras, not to the street: you're unreadable to the system and suspicious to anyone with eyes. "The same capability" means any equivalent recognition — another ghost mask, a cyberoptic with the facial recognition module — and against that there's no CM advantage: when the tech ties, the fiction decides.
