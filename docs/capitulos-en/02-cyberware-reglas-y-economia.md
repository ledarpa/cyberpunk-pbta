# Cyberware — Rules and Economy

## Chrome (implanted cyberware)

A chrome piece is any implant that replaces or upgrades part of your body and stays integrated in you. It modifies rolls, unlocks actions, and marks how far you can go in Night City.

Unlike hardware (gear you carry on you), chrome:

- You don't take it off in the middle of a scene.

- Each piece counts toward your implant load.

- They drive the loss of humanity and the @Psyche / cyberpsychosis risk.

### How to Read This Chapter

- Rules (this page): humanity, boosts, @Psyche, Neurodata, Stars and Credits.

- Catalog: implants and modules.

If it's your first session, read the rules before the catalog. In campaign play, the catalog is quick reference.

## Chrome, Humanity, and Cyberpsychosis

Chrome gives you capabilities beyond the natural body: extreme reflexes, augmented strength, amplified senses, a direct link to technology.

Every implant has a cost: the brain integrates digital and mechanical signals all the time. The more chrome, the more the mind disconnects from the human. In Night City that's called losing your humanity.

---

### Attribute boosts

Many chrome pieces raise one or more attributes (NE, CM, KR, MT). Boost points cost per this scale:

| Attribute bonus | Boost points |
| --- | --- |
| +1 | 1 |
| +2 | 2 |
| +3 | 4 |
| +4 | 8 |
| +5 | 16 |
| +6 | 32 |

Cap: the sum of chrome bonuses can't push any attribute above +4.

A single chrome piece can split several boost points across different attributes. Each implant counts as one installed chrome piece, even if it grants several benefits.

---

### Neural degradation

More implants = more mental wear.

- For every 3 installed chrome pieces, the character drops 1 point from an attribute of their choice (minimum −4).

- Each time they apply that penalty, mark 1 @Psyche box on the sheet.

---

### Cyberpsychosis episodes

Whoever wears chrome can suffer cyberpsychosis episodes.

- On a fumble with an attribute boosted by chrome: a 1-turn cyberpsychosis episode + mark 1 more @Psyche box.

According to the @Psyche level on the sheet:

| @Psyche boxes | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **1 to 3** | Brief episodes |
| **4 or 5** | **Major** cyberpsychosis for the **whole scene** |
| **More than 5** | **Permanent cyberpsycho** — the character passes to GM control |

> **Example — A cyberpsychosis episode**
> The bar fight is going fine until Kessler rolls to dodge and the dice spit out a **fumble**. With the combat arm boosting his KR, that means one thing: an episode. One turn. One more @Psyche box.
>
> And from the inside it feels like this:
>
> "My arm stops being mine in the middle of the movement. I want to grab the bottle and the arm decides no: it dodges, spins, and drives its elbow into the throat of the guy standing behind MY back, one I hadn't even seen. I feel the fingers closing around someone's neck on their own. I squeeze. No. *It squeezes.* I'm behind my own eyes, screaming in a room with no doors, looking out a window that is my face. The arm hums a low, content frequency, like an old vacuum cleaner. When it hands me back control there's a guy on the floor and everyone is looking at me the way you look at a dog that just talked."
>
> One turn. Three seconds of being a passenger in your own flesh.
>
> Afterward, in the bar's bathroom, Kessler marks the box on the sheet with the hand that still shakes. The other hand, the chrome one, is calm. Perfectly calm. That's the worst part.

### Getting your humanity back

There are two ways to lower @Psyche:

#### 1. Removing a chrome piece

The character can take an implant out on purpose.

- They lose all of that chrome piece's capabilities.

- Erases 1 @Psyche box.

- Reinstalling it without proper treatment raises @Psyche again.

- Improvised chrome must be destroyed to be removed mid-conflict; afterward a biohacker can repair it.

> **Example — Removing a chrome piece on purpose**
> Yuri's workshop, again. But this time there's no new box on the table. There's a sheet with @Psyche boxes marked, and a decision already made.
>
> —The eyes —says Mara, Artist, pointing at her own face—. I want you to take them out.
>
> Yuri doesn't ask. The Kirigami optics had cost her ten Credits and three years of seeing the world in layers: spectra, cables, lies. They had also cost her the nights, the dreams, the habit of looking people in the face instead of through them.
>
> The procedure is an installation in reverse: instead of adding, it subtracts. Yuri disconnects, unscrews, and sets both eyes down on a steel tray, where they lie staring at the ceiling with an insect's patience.
>
> Mara loses everything those lenses gave her. She'll never again see a body's heat through a wall or read fear in a corpo's pupil. In exchange, one @Psyche box gets erased from the sheet, like a debt settled.
>
> When she opens her new eyes —flesh, tears, limits— the workshop looks blurry and small. Beautiful.
>
> —And now? —asks Yuri.
>
> —Now I cry —says Mara, and she cries, and it comes out right, because the tears don't pass through any filter anymore.

#### 2. Digital psychological treatment

Therapy and specialized neural stabilization.

- 1 day of treatment → erases 1 @Psyche box.

- Recovering the 4th or 5th @Psyche level → 1 week of advanced treatment — and it always leaves one permanent disorder (narrative, or the GM's call).

## Progression: Neurodata, Stars and Credits

![](assets/manual/mejoras.png)

In Night City, intense experiences sell. Corporations, labs, and platforms pay for memories loaded with adrenaline, fear, and real emotion.

After a mission or a heavy scene, you can download the experience into a compatible terminal. That file is Neurodata: memories, sensations, and full perception, ready for storage and resale.

A Virtual Appraiser evaluates the Neurodata and pays in Stars. 1 Star = 1 Credit toward chrome, hardware, and upgrades.

> Selling experiences gives you power; every new chrome piece also brings you closer to losing your humanity. Economy and @Psyche are linked.

> **Example — Selling Neurodata**
> The Appraiser's bunker is three floors underground, in a spot that used to be a video store and still smells of nineties plastic. The crew arrives with the mission done and the adrenaline still warm.
>
> Sable, Spook, sits in the chair. The electrodes bite her temples. On the screen, the Virtual Appraiser: a mannequin face with a bank's smile.
>
> —Downloading experience —it says, in elevator voice—. Evaluating.
>
> And then the dizziness starts: the whole mission draining out through the back of your neck. The fear in the duct, the shot, the taste of blood when you bit down on the bullet. Everything that was yours is now a file. Merchandise.
>
> The Appraiser blinks.
>
> —Subject survived the mission. **One Star.** Acquired new, relevant knowledge: the mole's identity. **One Star.** Group's main objective: met. **One Star.** And the rooftop scene, with the helicopter… —the mannequin face pauses, almost like pleasure—. The players picked it as the session's Star Scene. **One more Star.**
>
> Four Stars. Four Credits, deposited with a digital chime.
>
> Sable pulls the electrodes off and stays there a moment staring at the wall, trying to remember what the fear in the duct smelled like. She still remembers, but only vaguely. She sold it. It's on a server somewhere, waiting for some bored corpo to live it first-person from an armchair.
>
> —Thank you for your experience —says the Appraiser as it deposits the credits into her account—. Come back soon.
>
> And you come back. You always come back.

### How to Earn Stars

At the end of a mission or a significant session, the Appraiser can give 1 Star per criterion met:

![](assets/manual/creditos.png)

- ★ The character survives the mission.

- ★ The character learns something new or relevant.

- ★ The group meets the main objective.

- ★ The character takes part in the session's Star Scene.

The players choose the Star Scene at the end: the session's most memorable or striking moment.

*(The 5 character creation stars are spent under the same Credit rules below.)*

---

### Credits

- Each Star = 1 Credit.

- Credits are earned by selling Neurodata; they're the main currency for chrome and hardware.

- You can hold Credits until you spend them.

---

### How to Spend Credits

#### New Cyberware (chrome or hardware)

Includes one basic subsystem for that piece.

| Quality | Cost in Credits |
| --- | --- |
| Improvised | 5 |
| Street | 10 |
| Hi-Tech | 15 |
| Military | 20 |

> **Example — Installing a chrome piece**
> Yuri the biohacker's office smells of alcohol, soldering iron, and stubbed-out cigarettes. Fifteen Credits on the steel table: a **Hi-Tech reflex amplifier**, still in its sealed box, with the Militech logo scraped off.
>
> —Sure about this? —asks Yuri, warming up the sonic scalpel—. After this, your spine isn't entirely yours anymore.
>
> —It stopped being mine three months ago. Go.
>
> You lie face down. The anesthesia is partial: it numbs the pain, not the consciousness. You feel the cold of the incision along your spine, the electric tickle of each vertebra being read, mapped, written. Yuri murmurs numbers like someone praying. Something threads into your marrow with a kiss of static.
>
> —Don't get up fast —he says, stitching—. The body takes a while to accept that it answers to two masters now.
>
> When you sit up, the world moves different. Slower. The fly buzzing by the lamp now flies in frames, and your hand catches it before you've finished deciding to. You open your fist. You watch it leave.
>
> Yuri counts the Credits. You count the seconds the machine just gave you, and somewhere in the back of your skull, a brand-new little voice starts talking with a factory accent.

#### Upgrading cyberware you already have

You hand in the previous version and pay only the difference in Credits between tiers. The replacement adds no extra wear or penalties.

#### Subsystems, accessories, and consumables

1 Credit each:

- A new subsystem

- An accessory

- A pack of 8 grenades

- A clip of exotic ballistic ammo

The GM can use the same cost for similar consumables or accessories if it fits the campaign.
