# Creating a Cyberpunk

## Create Your Cyberpunk

Here you define who you are at the start: archetype, starting attributes, and the gear you hit the street with.

You don't build a character à la carte: you pick an archetype with history, contacts, and limits. Every profession solves problems a different way (business, violence, information, art, espionage, nomad life…), but they all play by the same rules inside the System.

### What You Get at Creation

- Starting traits — The profession's `NE`, `CM`, `KR` and `MT` values are your starting modifiers (added to 2d6).

- Starting cyberware and hardware — Per *Starting Gear* below.

- Starting arsenal — One weapon from your profession's list.

- Creation stars — 5 stars under the same campaign rules (*Cyberware → Neurodata, Stars and Credits*): to acquire or upgrade a piece of hardware or chrome.

Pick a profession, write it on the sheet, and use the background as a guide (it adds no rules unless the GM agrees to them).

## Starting Gear

Starting cyberware: 1 improvised Neuraslot, 2 improvised chrome pieces or 1 street-grade chrome piece, plus 2 improvised hardware pieces or 1 street-grade hardware piece that isn't a weapon.

Starting arsenal: 1 street-grade weapon from those tied to your profession (list under each one).

Resources: 5 stars for accessories, a new chrome piece, or upgrading one you already have.

### Fixer

**At the table:** NE [+1] · CM [+1] · KR [-1] · MT [-1]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol, shotgun or assault rifle.

You started at the bottom: corner enforcer, small-time middleman, the guy who knew who was selling what in which alley. Night City doesn't reward loyalty; it rewards whoever understands other people's hunger. You saw the pattern before everyone else: every desire has a price, every fear has a buyer, and whoever connects both sides keeps the margin. You're not a neighborhood hero. You're the bridge the Street crosses when the Corporation won't pick up the phone.

These days your merchandise isn't just stolen metal. Guns crossing borders, "misplaced" med-packs, chips with firmware nobody should have. If the market doesn't exist, you invent it: a shortage told right, a rumor in the right club, a favor collected three weeks later. You know how to create demand because you know how to read desperation. And when someone calls you *Fixer*, it's not a compliment: it's a verbal contract with blood in the fine print.

Your real capital doesn't fit in a safe. It's a network: mercs who need representation, netrunners who want a client that won't sell them out, outsiders moving cargo without asking too many questions. You buy and sell favors like a currency trader. A nightspot in your name. A bribe that opens a dock. A truce between two crews that still hate each other… brokered by you, billed by you. When a corporate war ends, somebody has to divide up what's left. That somebody is usually a Fixer.

Some days you hide a fugitive, sneak food through a blockade, or put a roof over someone who can't pay. It's not charity: it's investment. In a city made of fragments, you're the wire splicing what the system cut apart. The working poor don't get through certain doors; you do. And every open door leaves a debit. You'll collect. You always collect. Because in Night City the only ethic that survives is the fair price… as defined by whoever sets it.

> **Example — Fixer**
> The meet is at the club. Haze, pink neon, the buyer flanked by two bodyguards in cheap hardware.
>
> **Player:** "I make him believe there's another buyer waiting outside. I want him to pay double and walk away thinking he won."
>
> **GM:** "That's reading people and selling them smoke. Roll CM."
>
> 2d6 + 1. Comes up **8**. Partial success.
>
> **GM:** "He buys the story and pays double, but choose: you end up owing the club owner a favor, the buyer demands to meet your 'supplier' in person, or one of the gorillas recognizes you from a deal that went bad in Heywood."
>
> **Player:** "The favor. The club owner writes me into his ledger."
>
> **Player (narrates):** "I lean over the table and drop my voice, like it hurts to say what comes next. 'Look, the Watson guy is offering twenty over and pushing me for an answer. If we close now, I leave him hanging.' I watch his eyes do the math. He signs the transfer with the smile of a man who thinks he won. On my way out, the club owner stops me at the door with two fingers on my shoulder. 'Nice show. You owe me.' I nod. In my mental ledger, the debt already has a due date and three ways to collect it without opening my wallet."

### Artist

**At the table:** NE [-1] · CM [+1] · KR [+1] · MT [-1]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol or sniper rifle.

You don't sing to decorate a bar. You sing —or film, or paint walls the cops wash off at dawn— because the Street still needs a voice that doesn't come with a corporate logo. You're this era's Rockerboy: asphalt poet, agitator, a conscience bleeding through amplifiers and hijacked screens. With pocket studios and satellites anyone can hack on a given night, your message travels farther than any official speech. And that terrifies the people who actually run things.

The Corporations and the Government would rather you were background noise: fashion, nostalgia, product. You'd rather be a problem. A song that names what nobody wants named. A clip that shows the body under the skyscraper. A show that empties couches and fills streets. You know there are bloodlines behind you: squealing guitars, honest lyrics, raw footage that tore down bigger lies. You don't pretend to be a saint. You pretend not to shut up while you still have a stage… or a rooftop.

The power you handle isn't a rifle's, but it moves rifles. One piece of yours can push a shy kid onto the barricade and a coward into looking people in the eye. Legendary Artists led riots against glass towers and exposed dictators with three chords and a stolen camera. That's too much weight for someone sleeping in a different greenroom every week. You carry it anyway, because silence also kills: just slower.

Night City offers you contracts, sponsors, and a golden cage with great sound. You'll take just enough not to starve and refuse whatever turns you into a jingle. You know they'll threaten you, buy you, clone your style. That they'll love you in public and hate you in private. You keep going: every night the same pact with the audience —say something true before they kill the lights—. If the system wants decorative artists, let it hire mannequins. You came to make noise that hurts.

> **Example — Artist**
> The club has no name, just a wall. And the wall has your face on it, projected from a satellite you stole bandwidth from — property of an insurance corpo. Below, two hundred people who got off work two hours ago and still can't afford dinner. You sing them what they already know: that the tower next door has filtered air and elevators that never come down this far.
>
> The first song is anger. The second, grief. By the third, the cellphones stop recording you and start broadcasting — not for you, ==against== the corpo. That's the difference: you're not content, you're the hole in the screen the street crawls through.
>
> Tomorrow the cops erase the mural. Tomorrow the corpo buys an algorithm so your name never shows up in any feed. Tomorrow. Today there's a girl in the front row who learned the lyrics and will sing them at a march in a month, and she'll never know where they came from. That's how it works. You don't sign anything. You leave your voice lit up in the city like a window left open in summer: to let the air in, even if the bullets come in too.

### Biohacker

**At the table:** NE [+1] · CM [-1] · KR [0] · MT [0]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol or dart launcher.

If anything near you stays intact for more than five minutes, it's because you haven't opened it yet. Screwdrivers in your pockets, solder smell in your clothes, a head full of schematics nobody ever drew in an official manual. In a world where half the city runs on tech nobody fully understands, you're the one who still dares to look inside. You fix the impossible out of habit. The legal stuff, when it's convenient. The illegal stuff, when it pays.

Small jobs keep you alive: a Metrocar that won't start, a HUD bleeding static, an interface that refuses to talk to the rest of the body. Big jobs make you dangerous: weapons that shouldn't exist, stolen chrome, espionage kits for black ops, thermite, hunter drones, toys that end up at funerals. You charge well because the black market has no warranty department. And you spend better: every credit comes back as tools, data, and prototypes that will be contraband tomorrow.

Your clients don't always love you afterward. People walk around with your mods and hate you for what you made them able to do… or for what failed on them at three in the morning. That's why your workshop looks like a makeshift fortress and your list of trusted mercs is never empty. In Night City, the Tech who survives isn't the brightest: it's the one who assumes somebody will come to collect a technical debt in lead.

You have a cousin —a brother of the trade— who cuts flesh and cable with the same calm: the Ripperdoc. While you resurrect a toaster, he's cracking chests open in basements and seating implants Trauma Team will pretend not to have seen. You both know the same secret: here medicine is already mechanical, and mechanics is already politics. He's hoping a "clean" contract saves him. You're hoping you won't need one too soon. Pious lie. In this city, sooner or later everyone comes back to the same workbench… or the same gurney.

> **Example — Biohacker**
> The client's ocular implant is throwing blue sparks and the building's elevator is locked down by a security ICE that isn't in any catalog.
>
> **Player:** "I open the elevator panel and bridge the firmware with my kit. Improvised, like always."
>
> **GM:** "NE, clearly. Roll."
>
> 2d6 + 1. Comes up **5**. Miss.
>
> **GM:** "Pick the mess: the short circuit burns out your work kit, the ICE copies your signature and now there's a trace of you on the building's network, or the client's implant shuts down completely and he needs a clandestine operating room right now."
>
> **Player:** "The trace. My signature is on the network."
>
> **Player (narrates):** "I stick my fingers into the panel, cross two cables that should never have touched, and the elevator coughs, climbs half a floor… and dies. The diagnostic screen spits out a log that shouldn't exist: my solder signature, my voltage pattern, all of it uploaded to the building's network like a neon fingerprint. I kill the workshop lights. 'Change of plans,' I tell the client, who blinks his good eye. 'We're moving. Now.' Behind the door I can already hear the hum of a security drone that learned my name."

### Media

**At the table:** NE [0] · CM [+1] · KR [-1] · MT [0]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol.

They manufacture custom-fit truth. You take it apart live. The Corporations dump toxic waste, buy judges, unleash proxy wars, and smile in the screamsheets. The Government doesn't stop them: it's just another client. The last dike between total monopoly and a Street that's still half-awake is the Media… and you're in that trench with a press pass, contacts at every rave, and an audience that still believes —or pretends to— that information can hurt the ones on top.

When you dig, the cover-up arrives first: officials, lawyers, lapdogs in suits. Then the threats. Then the attempt to wipe you off the air. Once they canceled your entire netblog; you came back with your team and pushed the story until Management had to swallow it. You've had too many guns pointed at you. That's why a mercenary walks in your shadow and a good netrunner —expensive, jittery, indispensable— digs through the Net while you look into the camera. Being brave isn't enough. You have to be better than the corporate forgetting machine.

Last week: a pharma outfit selling poison on the Street in medical packaging. This week: a secret war down south with cyber-troops and body counts no official bulletin will ever admit to. Tomorrow it might be a "possibly nuclear" shipment headed to a port nobody wants to name. Every lead is a detonator. Every broadcast is a bet that your face stays on screen and not in a ditch.

You know your own media Corporation uses you as much as you use it. Sponsors, ratings, "editorial" limits. You play that game without forgetting the other one: the Street's game, where people need names, evidence, and someone saying out loud what everyone whispers. If you go quiet, the silence isn't neutral: it's an accomplice. So you load the gear, warn your backup, and go out again. Exposing bastards isn't a slogan. It's how you keep from becoming one.

> **Example — Media**
> LIVE — CHANNEL WITH NO ASSIGNED FREQUENCY — 03:47
>
> The camera shakes because the ground is shaking. Behind it, the Arasaka depot fire lights up half the city like a dawn nobody asked for. She doesn't run. The mercenary at her side runs; she walks forward, mic in hand, jacket with the netblog logo crossed out in silver tape.
>
> —What you're seeing there is not an industrial accident —she says, and her voice comes out whole, trained in a thousand erased broadcasts—. It's an inventory that stopped being profitable with people still inside. I have the cargo manifests. I have the shift schedules. I have the names.
>
> A security drone crosses the frame. Behind it, a mercenary raises his rifle point-blank; she pushes the barrel down with two fingers, never breaking eye contact with the camera. She doesn't want martyrs, she wants audience.
>
> —Before they cut my signal —and they will cut it— the files are already replicated on four hundred nodes. Erasing me erases nothing.
>
> The screen fills with static. Three seconds. When the image comes back, she's still there, smiling like someone who already won tomorrow's edition.
>
> —As I was saying.

### Corpo

**At the table:** NE [0] · CM [+1] · KR [0] · MT [-1]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol.

In another century they'd have called you a yuppie with an MBA and a catalog smile. Today you're an Exec: soul mortgaged to the Company, access to floors where the air gets filtered and the morals don't. Corporations don't influence the cyberpunk world. They administer it. Governments, markets, armies, low orbit. Whoever controls the tower controls the board. You got in to climb. You still haven't decided whether it was ambition or fear of staying down.

Junior executive life isn't an open floor plan with free coffee. It's a war of hallways. The ones below would kill for your chair. The ones above would kill to keep you from theirs. Sabotage, bribery, blackmail, elevator "accidents": routine. Every young wolf keeps mercs and netrunners the way you keep a life insurance policy… or a gun under the desk. The prize is real power. The punishment is the Street, or an autopsy nobody will investigate.

Your projects swing between gray and monstrous. A productivity plan for the medical subsidiary. A black team seeding a designer plague so Marketing can sell the cure. Talent hunting: kidnapping the rival's researcher. Stealing the blueprints for a suborbital shuttle to copy it and resell it to the highest geostrategic bidder. You sign orders other people execute. You sleep telling yourself "someone else pulled the trigger." The city doesn't register that subtlety.

You tell yourself you got in to change the Corporation from the inside. Every quarter that line sounds hollower. Ethics are a luxury for people who don't have a report due in an hour and a Sales colleague planning to freeze your database —and your career— for good. In the executive dining room they talk about synergies; in the basements, about bodies. You don't confuse the map with the territory anymore. You're going to move first. Not because you're the movie's villain. Because on the thirtieth floor, the only narrative that matters is whoever's still breathing with an active badge… and a black team on the other side of the line, waiting for your OK.

> **Example — Corpo**
> Boardroom, floor 88. Your director just presented your project as her own in front of the committee.
>
> **Player:** "I don't confront her. I ask her a technical question I know she can't answer, wearing my best collaborator smile. Let the committee notice on their own."
>
> **GM:** "Fine hallway play. CM."
>
> 2d6 + 1. Comes up **11**. Full success.
>
> **Player (narrates):** "I raise my hand with the face of a model employee. 'Sorry, director, just to clarify: does the Q3 figure already discount the Osaka supplier leak, or does that go in the appendix?' Silence. She doesn't know there was a leak in Osaka. I know because I provoked it three weeks ago, with a memo she signed. The committee starts leafing through the report. She mumbles something about 'reviewing it later.' The vice president looks at me for the first time in two years —he looks at *me*— and writes something on his tablet. When the meeting ends, my director asks for a word in the hallway. I smile. On floor 88, smiles collect interest, and she just went into debt."

### Spook

**At the table:** NE [-1] · CM [0] · KR [+1] · MT [0]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol or dart launcher.

You enlisted believing in flags. Country, order, "somebody has to do it." After a dozen jobs, flags look like older logos: they still order deaths, but they no longer explain why. You stayed in the trade, because the trade rewrites you. You've spent months with someone else's name, someone else's clothes, someone else's laugh. Not to show off. To be exactly where the file says a ghost with a pulse is needed.

You're not the movie-poster spy. The ones who survive don't shine: they dissolve. They trained you to stack objectives into a single infiltration: pull a file, mark a target, open a door someone else will walk through with a rifle. Assassination, kidnapping, data extraction, long surveillance in a complex reeking of ozone and lies. Changing identity isn't a party costume: it's social surgery. You sink into the place's routine until the place treats you like furniture. That's when you finally steal what you came to steal.

Your employers swap masks: state agencies that no longer control their own yard, corporate intel divisions, brokers selling secrets to the highest bidder. Sometimes the "right side" sends you to protect an asset. Sometimes to bury it. You learned not to ask out loud. Asking shows. And what shows, kills. Your worst enemy isn't the lobby guard: it's your reflection in a pane of glass at 03:14, when you forget for a second which name you were supposed to answer to.

The body pays the price the report never mentions. Discreet implants, paranoia habits, friendships that are cover legends. You sleep in borrowed beds and wake up running escape routes in your head. If you fail, there's no funeral with a flag: there's a gap in a database and a replacement with your same haircut. You go back in anyway. Because in Night City information is ammunition, and somebody is going to fire it. You'd rather be the finger on the trigger —or the one bending the barrel— than the face on the poster on the wall. Quiet. Precise. Almost out of ideals… and more dangerous for it.

> **Example — Spook**
> He's been Marcelo for four months. Marcelo works in complex logistics, lunches alone, nods hello, and nobody remembers what he talked about yesterday. Marcelo is furniture with a credential.
>
> Today Marcelo photocopies. Nobody watches the guy who photocopies. The file goes through the scanner, and from the scanner into a chip sewn into his shirt lining. Sixteen seconds. At second twelve the supervisor walks in and asks if he saw the coffee from office three. He says no. He wishes her a good weekend. She leaves. Second sixteen: file complete, machine rebooted, log wiped.
>
> In the parking lot he leaves a paper under the wiper of a gray car. Nobody will see who picks it up. The paper says only: *north door, Thursday, 02:00*. Another team will walk through that door with rifles in six days and never know who opened it.
>
> At night, in the apartment that isn't his, he takes Marcelo's accent off in front of the mirror. It takes effort. He can't quite remember how he used to laugh. He writes in his ciphered notebook: *objective met, cover intact*. He doesn't write that he liked the supervisor. That doesn't go in any report. That buries itself.

### Outsider

**At the table:** NE [0] · CM [-1] · KR [0] · MT [+1]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol, shotgun, assault rifle or sniper rifle.

When the financial collapse swept away jobs and homes, the highways filled with people who no longer had a mailing address. Riots, curfews, corporations buying up whatever the State couldn't hold. Out of that exodus something was born that the city still eyes sideways: the Nomads, the outsiders. They're not tourists. They're a people in motion, bound by shared debt, blood, and the certainty that standing still is dying by inches.

Your life is the convoy, the clan, the pact that nobody gets left without a wheel at three in the morning. Families, clans, tribes, nations: scales of loyalty more real than any citizenship. The Seven Nations have leaders, routes, and pride. The Raffen Shiv are the shadow on the asphalt: pariahs, raiders, proof that even among nomads there are those who only speak the language of the loot. You know which side of the line you walk… or you pretend not to know when the job calls for it.

You take contracts because the pack needs fuel, food, and parts: legal transport, smuggling, harvest season, labor on builds nobody else wants. While some run jobs, others watch the elders, the kids, and the engines. Then the tents come down and the camp is dust in the rearview again. You live on the edge of the law: you trade in stolen goods, help fugitives, comb ruins and abandoned bases. Hitting an entire corporate convoy is usually madness… except for those with nothing left to lose.

In Night City you're the smell of oil and desert that comes in when someone opens the wrong garage door. They need you to move what shouldn't be seen, and to remember the world doesn't end at the neon ring. Your family is your nation. Your vehicle, your home. Your word is sometimes worth more than a corpo contract… and sometimes it breaks just as ugly. You stay on the road because the road doesn't lie: you move forward together, or the highway swallows the stragglers.

> **Example — Outsider**
> Tell us the flood story again, grandma. Come on, the kids never heard it.
>
> All right. The flood took the road, the trucks, and half the Okafor family. The city, three hundred kilometers away, didn't even send a drone to look. You know who came? The Vargas, who owed us nothing. Forty pickup trucks, chains, gas canisters, and a kitchen built in the mud that fed two hundred people for a week. That's where I learned what *nation* means: it's not a passport. It's whoever shows up when the water rises.
>
> The city offers you something else. It offers a contract, chrome on an installment plan, a roof with cameras. And when you're used up, it offers you the street. We don't have a street: we have the road, and the road belongs to everyone or it belongs to no one. Whoever's left without a wheel at three in the morning doesn't call a tow truck —he whistles, and the convoy stops. It always stops.
>
> Are there rats among us? Yes. The Raffen Shiv are children of the exodus too, but they chose to loot the ones walking beside them. That's why, when you see dust on the horizon, look twice before you smile. The desert doesn't tell us apart. We do.

### Mercenary

**At the table:** NE [-1] · CM [-1] · KR [+1] · MT [+1]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol, shotgun, assault rifle or sniper rifle + improvised pistol.

You were born with a gun in your flesh hand. The other hand —metal, the one that builds death with catalog precision— came later, when the original body wasn't enough anymore. You're a Solo: hired muscle, enforcer, contract cybersoldier. Whether under a corporate flag or in "police actions" that never get called wars, the trade is the same: turning violence into billable output.

Most of yours came out of private armies or official campaigns that left more chrome than medals behind. Every wound pushes in more hardware: weapon-limbs, armor under the skin, chips sharpening reflexes, combat drugs buying seconds at the cost of years. If you climb to the top of the samurai ladder, maybe you make ronin: assassin, escort, or enforcer for whoever pays your obscene rate. The market loves the best. The market also discards them without a funeral.

The price isn't poetry. It's addiction, paranoia, and a body you no longer quite recognize as yours. Your instincts are so trained that calm feels like a system failure. You don't trust mothers, lovers, or "band of brothers": in this line of work betrayal is a fringe benefit. One night you sleep in a penthouse watching lit towers. The next, in an alley that smells of ozone and dried blood. Same person. Same trigger. Different receipt.

You know the Street looks at you like a walking weapon… and it isn't entirely wrong. You also know that without people like you, Execs don't get out of the elevator and Media don't make it to broadcast alive. You don't ask to be loved. You ask to be paid up front, and for the contract to say clearly who dies if somebody improvises. Deep down, everyone is buying the same thing: the illusion of control through your trigger. Being the best isn't glory. It's surviving one more night being exactly what Night City built: a lethal solution with a name on the invoice.

> **Example — Mercenary**
> Simple contract: hold the hallway for three minutes while the client evacuates. Four hostiles, heavy weapons, already rounding the corner.
>
> **Player:** "I hold the position. Suppressing fire, cover, no hero stuff."
>
> **GM:** "Under fire: timing and nerve. KR."
>
> 2d6 + 1. Comes up **9**. Partial success.
>
> **GM:** "You hold the three minutes, but: you eat a burst and the dermal armor ends up shredded, the rifle mag runs dry and you're down to your sidearm, or one hostile flanks you and stays loose inside the building."
>
> **Player:** "The armor. Let the body pay — that's what it's for."
>
> **Player (narrates):** "First burst at the corner, two of them peek, two drop. I change positions like I was taught: never twice from the same hole. The HUD timer reads 1:40. A burst catches my side and the subcutaneous armor shuts off with a click — I feel the heat, not the pain; that comes later. Doesn't matter. 2:30. I give them the hallway meter by meter, never my back. 3:00. The client's in the air. I fall back stepping through puddles of my own coolant. The report will say: objective met, acceptable material damage. The material damage is me. It gets invoiced anyway."

### Netrunner

**At the table:** NE [+1] · CM [0] · KR [0] · MT [-1]. **Starting arsenal:** pistol.

You started early: an old machine, a screen that made your eyes water, a kid's certainty that the real world was a badly designed interface. In school you were already bending systems the adults thought were secure. At thirteen you moved enough money —out of accounts nobody was watching properly— to buy your first interfaces. Since then, the body is a modem with lungs. The Net, your real weather.

Now you dive local data currents and, when you've got the guts or the debt, you peek into the old global mesh that still connects what's left of wired humanity. You're a ghost: offensive and defensive programs one thought away, digital demons and vampires that open corporate fortresses like cans. You've been inside where the logos promise invulnerability. You've bought, traded, and sold secrets that would end careers… or cities.

Sometimes you stumble on betrayals, experiments, and mass graves in file format. That's not what keeps you in. What keeps you in is the next ICE, the next satellite, the next architecture nobody's mapped yet. You know the anti-intruder AIs get better every cycle, and that one day a faster system will snag you by the connectors and switch your heart off the way you close a session. You keep diving anyway. The race only ends when the biological clock —or the firewall— decides you've run enough.

On the Street you're the specialist everyone wants to hire and nobody wants seated at the table for too long: too much power, too much silence, too much chance you're reading their implants while they smile at you. You charge in stars, chrome, and access. You pay in deferred humanity. Every dive is a pact: a little closer to the god of information, a little farther from the animal that used to sweat in front of that first screen. Your flesh friends learn not to ask what you saw in there. You learn not to tell. And still, you jack in. Because outside there's only asphalt. Inside, there's still horizon.

> **Example — Netrunner**
> Biotechnica's data fortress glows on the Net like a tower of black glass. The file you want is in the basement.
>
> **Player:** "I go in through the maintenance node. I deploy the vampire, silent, touching nothing else."
>
> **GM:** "NE. And may the Net like you."
>
> 2d6 + 1. Comes up **12**. Full success.
>
> **Player (narrates):** "I slip out of the body the way you take off a jacket. The Net receives me with its usual weather: cold currents, floating garbage, the heartbeat of a million machines. The vampire comes out of my wrist — my code, my baby, sharpened for months — and attaches to Biotechnica's perimeter like a well-mannered leech. The ICE patrols three meters from my face and can't smell me. I follow the maintenance duct down to the basement, and there it is: the file, wrapped in corporate encryption that dissolves like sugar in rain. I copy, I pull out, I lock the door from the outside. When I get back to the body, in the grimy chair of my cube, only eleven seconds have passed. My hands are shaking. I'm smiling. Somewhere up in Biotechnica, tomorrow, someone is going to find out the black glass had a window open."

> **Example — Building a cyberpunk, live**
> The table smells of cold coffee. Vale, twenty-two, first session, brings a single line: "My mother was a biohacker. I want the street."
>
> —So you're torn between Biohacker and Fixer —says the GM—. Think of it this way: the Fixer solves things with contacts and prices; the Biohacker opens the machine and fixes it with her hands. What do you do when something breaks?
>
> —I open it.
>
> —Biohacker, then.
>
> *Flash: a workshop in Watson, her mother soldering over a bench that's hers now.*
>
> Vale writes down the profession's traits: **NE +1, CM −1, KR 0, MT 0**. She reads them out loud, like getting inked.
>
> —Starting cyberware —the GM goes on—. One **improvised Neuraslot**, which doesn't add @Psyche, and on top of that you choose: two improvised chrome pieces or one street-grade.
>
> —Street-grade. I want something my mother would have signed off on.
>
> *Flash: the clandestine clinic, the ripperdoc holding the implant up against the light. "This isn't alley junk. Your mother built this."*
>
> —Hardware: two improvised pieces or one street-grade that isn't a weapon.
>
> —Two improvised —says Vale—. My mother's repair kit, the backpack that weighs like a dead man, and a first aid kit for the basement.
>
> *Flash: the backpack dropping onto her shoulder the first night, tools ringing like keys to a house that no longer exists.*
>
> —Starting arsenal: from the Biohacker's list, pistol or dart launcher.
>
> —Dart launcher. If I have to shoot, let it be with anesthesia.
>
